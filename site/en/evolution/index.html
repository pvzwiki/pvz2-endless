Evolution: how its results are chosen—and why they are predictableArtifacts & accessories

Evolution's choices follow a reproducible shuffle sequence. In the recorded checks, the same starting conditions produced the same results, and predictions fixed before play matched subsequent checks. The candidate list, planting order, and previews explain how that happens.

A repeatable sequence behind the choice

The picker builds an ordered candidate list, shuffles the whole list, and takes its first plant. It uses the library's two-argument std::random_shuffle, backed by an mt19937 engine starting from seed 5489. Captured raw outputs and full permutations replay from that state.01

The engine belongs to the process. A full game restart returns it to its initial sequence. Earlier shuffles advance it, including the Evolution previews on the artifact screen. The documented direct-entry routes begin at the state reached after those previews; restarting a level did not advance or reset it in the recorded checks.02

A pseudorandom algorithm can be useful without exposing a predictable sequence. Here, the fixed starting state and shared preview history make the recorded outcomes reproducible in advance. The problem is more specific than occasionally seeing the same plant twice.

Each source has its own candidate list

The registry order matters. Configured names in PlantTypeOrder come first, followed by the remaining types in the observed fresh-load order. Shuffling the same plants in declaration order can select a different result, even with identical random outputs.03

Seven catalog filters leave 257 of the sample's 383 plant definitions. They remove disabled types, explicit aliases, vine classes, hero types, parallel_ types, consumables, and the artifact's result blacklist. The stage rule, level seed-bank bans, and the source cell's planting checks narrow the list further.04

The price test compares two different quantities:

candidate declared cost>source effective cost.\text{candidate declared cost}>\text{source effective cost}.candidate declared cost>source effective cost.

The source's effective price can include level adjustments, discounts, and a zero lower bound. The candidate uses its declared Cost. A discounted source can therefore produce a plant with a lower listed price than its own original listed price. At effective cost 500 or above, the sample's filtered catalog has no more expensive candidate; the source remains unchanged.05

In the calculator, effective sun costs are shared settings for starting plants. You can customize any number of plant types, independently of your wanted results. Each setup search uses up to three selected starting-plant types to keep the search manageable; prediction uses the plants placed on the board.

The cell also matters. In the recorded Memory Lane configuration, at effective source cost 0:

These are counts for that level and cost. The dry shore admits Lily Pad; a plant standing on a pad uses the pad restrictions whether the shore is dry or flooded. Those restrictions exclude Cactus. The flooded pool without a pad matches the declared CanLiveOnWaves flag in that capture. Equipped water or sky boosts can change the latter rule. Cob Cannon and Intensive Carrot have additional board prerequisites, so their exclusion in a fresh ordinary setup is not a universal ban.06

Planting order connects draws to cells

The active skill collects eligible plants overlapping its 3×3 area. Newly registered plant objects are inserted at the head of the live list, which the target query walks forwards. For freshly planted sources, the newest plant is therefore processed first.03

Reversing the planting order in the recorded Egypt 13 trial reversed which cells received the selections. This is registration order, not a fixed scan from one corner of the board. Moving or restoring an existing plant requires its actual registration history.

Each processed source consumes the complete shuffle for its own candidate list. Changing a cost or cell condition can change the list length, the result, and the stream position inherited by later sources. An empty list leaves the source in place without consuming shuffle outputs.

Previews advance the same stream

A rank-1 preview evolves nine Sunflowers from a 226-entry pool. The first such preview after a fresh launch consumed 2,874 raw outputs. That is the measured first preview, not a constant stride for every preview: rejection sampling makes consumption depend on the current state.02

The measured rank-4 preview performs three evolutions, three single-candidate Lily Pad selections, and six spawns from a 55-entry pool. The single-candidate selections consume no outputs. The spawn sequence was recorded; the cells receiving those six spawns were not.

Recorded prospective checks cover a reversed Egypt 13 planting order, preview routes in Egypt 8, requested results in Memory Lane and Penny's Pursuit, and a mixed rank-1/rank-4 preview sequence. The records distinguish captured selections from reported outcomes; confirming a requested plant does not establish every unrecorded cell.07

The pvzreport/evolution-artifact repository provides the reference implementation, level inputs, and recorded candidate-list fixtures. The web calculator offers prediction and setup search for its built-in story levels and the Terror from Tomorrow preset. Each preset supplies the stage, cell conditions and seed-bank restrictions; reproducing a calculation also requires the stated preview history and effective source costs.

Choosing a result and replacing a plant are separate steps

Selection creates a delayed effect carrying the chosen type, cell, and original plant level. On its animation event, the code removes the old plant, then checks whether the replacement can be planted. A failed final check skips the addition without restoring the source.08

Selection excuses blocked-reason 79; the final check does not. Board state can also change while the effect is pending. An empty candidate list is different: it creates no removal effect and leaves the original plant untouched.

Higher ranks have additional rules

The rank-2 rate getters use the 26-name TargetablePlantTypes whitelist, although the description expresses a 300-sun threshold. Eleven ordinary plants meeting that threshold are absent from the list. The getter membership check and its downstream effect on plant statistics are distinct evidence questions.09

Rank 3 has a timed spawn path. Rank 4 scans empty cells before the queued evolutions remove their sources and selects bonus plants costing at most 100. Its bonus-level calculation starts at 1 and takes a minimum, so a bank of higher-level packets cannot raise the result above 1. These additional in-level paths are outside the web calculator's rank-1 activation model.

The Evolution reference retains the declared formulas and plant lists.

Restoring unpredictable choices

The game should use randomness supplied by the operating system for Evolution's choices.

  8. 08Deferred replacement and unequal checks

    The effect stores a cell, replacement type, animation name, original level, and old-plant weak pointer. The loop event tags and kills the old plant before checking replacement placement. Selection excuses blocked-reason 79; the final check excuses no reason. Failure skips AddPlant, with no rollback in this path. This ordering is traced in native instructions; the picker captures did not instrument later insertion callbacks. Other source-plant state is not copied through this effect; AddPlant may obtain new state from the board or profile.

  9. 09Three separate passive paths

    The two rank-2 rate getters consult the 26-entry TargetablePlantTypes whitelist. The rank-3 timer advances one interval per due update and draws rows 0–4. Rank 4 scans empty cells before the deferred removals, selecting plants with Cost <= 100. Its level fold starts at 1 and takes a minimum, so ordinary positive packet levels cannot raise it. The report traces the rate getters; their callers into final plant statistics need separate attribution.
