<section id="wave-health" className="prose-section">

## Record health after the actions finish

At wave start, `WaveManager` invokes every action before recording `m_currentWaveTotalHealth`. The query filters entities by `m_spawnedFromWave` and a virtual state predicate. Its base health implementation converts body plus helmet HP to an integer when entity flag mask `0x2` is set, and returns zero otherwise.

Let $H_0$ be this initial reported current-wave total, and $C_{32}$ the signed 32-bit conversion detailed below. For a sampled fraction $q$, the stored integer threshold is

$$
\begin{aligned}
T&=C_{32}\!\left(F_{32}\!\left(F_{32}(H_0)F_{32}(q)\right)\right),\\
q&\in[0.7,0.85]
\end{aligned}
$$

for the constructor baseline. Later checks query remaining health $H(t)$ again. Neither point cost nor accumulated player damage substitutes for that query.<Note id="waveHealth" />

| Entity or change | Effect on this wave’s health calculation |
| --- | --- |
| Jam replacement and a successfully created leader | Their resulting health is present before the initial snapshot |
| Separate flag action | Its entity contributes if it passes the same wave/state filters |
| King/Fisherman rejected in the placement pass | Normally already excluded before $H_0$ is recorded |
| A counted entity dies or becomes excluded later | Can reduce $H(t)$ without rewriting $H_0$ or $T$ |
| Earlier-wave survivor | Different wave marker; excluded from this total |
| Portal child | Marker $-5$; excluded from this ordinary wave total |
| Zombie transported through a Steam pipe | Original wave marker retained; transport state is not excluded by the base filter |

For example, 8,000 eligible HP plus a 2,000-HP newcomer rejected during placement gives $H_0=8000$, and $q=0.8$ gives $T=6400$. It does not first record 10,000 HP and then credit rejection as 2,000 damage. If a counted zombie disappears afterward, the later remaining-health total can fall even without a plant attack.

<section id="hp-saturation" className="prose-section">

## A large HP report can reach its integer limit

The executable is ARM64, but this path combines `float32` arithmetic with signed 32-bit integer reports. The base getter adds body and helmet HP, then converts that sum to an integer. The wave query converts each reported contribution back to `float32`, adds the contributions in order, and converts the accumulator to an integer again.

For positive HP, $C_{32}$ truncates toward zero within range and saturates at $2^{31}-1=2,147,483,647$ above it. The conversion does not wrap a large positive report into a negative value. This describes the inspected non-trapping conversion behavior; the health fields themselves remain separate.<Note id="hpSaturation" />

Once the initial query saturates, the stored baseline stops growing with the uncapped accumulator. These thresholds include the native float32 rounding:

| Sampled fraction | Threshold after the initial report saturates |
| --- | ---: |
| 0.7 | 1,503,238,528 |
| 0.8 | 1,717,986,944 |
| 0.85 | 1,825,361,152 |

For a constructed starting accumulator of 10,000,000,000 HP and $q=0.8$, the remaining report must fall to about 1,717,986,944. That represents roughly 82.82% of the starting amount disappearing, rather than 20%. While the remaining report is also saturated, it is still above the smaller threshold. The ordinary timer can advance the wave earlier; saturation does not disable that timer.

The progress calculation also uses the reported current/initial health ratio. While both reports are saturated, the ratio remains 1 and the health-derived part of within-wave progress remains zero. If a single base getter reaches the same limit, only its reported contribution is capped; this query does not reduce its stored body or helmet HP.

Rounding can matter before saturation. A constructed sequence of 79 contributions of 27,000,000 produces 2,133,002,496 in the checked float32 accumulation. With 80 such contributions, the accumulator is 2,160,002,560 and the returned total is 2,147,483,647. The construction view now follows these reporting conversions for its eligible ordinary-action subtotal.

The <Link href="/strength/#strength-table">adopted Endless gameplay reference</Link> gives a level-10 health multiplier of 100,000. Its effective runtime configuration has not been captured. Using this reference, base HP 270 becomes 27,000,000. A wave’s point budget alone still does not establish its HP, and the final scheduled wave has no additional ordinary batch after it.

<section id="wave-clock" className="prose-section">

## The deadline can move forward

Let $t_0$ be the last-wave time, $I$ the selected interval, and initially $D=t_0+I$. The ordinary interval is 20 plus a floating draw in 0–5 game seconds, or 35 seconds when the following wave is large.

When the remaining-health test succeeds, the normal path updates the deadline as

$$
H(t)\le T\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D\leftarrow\min(D,t).
$$

Subject to the normal state/mode guards, its advance test then requires

$$
t\ge D\quad\land\quad t-t_0\ge4.
$$

Reaching the health threshold early therefore makes the deadline due, but does not remove this path’s four-second minimum. Even $H_0=T=0$ still passes through its timing guards.

If the next wave is large, the normal path first enters the announcement state and adds `HugeWaveDelay` to the existing deadline. The constructor delay is 5 seconds. It does not start an unrelated five-second timer from the announcement entry time. With $t_0=0$ and health reaching the threshold at $t=1$, the first normal gate opens at 4, but the shifted deadline is $1+5=6$. With no early health trigger, the 35-second deadline becomes 40.<Note id="waveClock" />

Startup has its own countdown: constructor values are 12 seconds normally and 5 for conveyor mode. A level/perk helper can modify the normal startup value. These are game-clock quantities, separate from the later intervals and from zombie movement pace.

</section>

<section id="automatic-next" className="prose-section">

## Automatic next-wave uses a direct request

`HasSkipNextWave` reacts when requested button visibility changes from false to true. The normal timed state requests visibility when

$$
t\ge D-I/2.
$$

A health-triggered earlier $D$ can therefore make the button available early. If the preference and boundary guards allow it, the Board sends the same request as a manual next-wave action. The ordinary request calls the advance function directly; it does not rerun the normal deadline, health, or four-second test.

For a current player-facing wave number $w$, regular flag spacing $s$, and a wave already started, the automatic boundary guard is

$$
w\bmod s=s-1.
$$

The handler also suppresses requests before the first wave. Its boundary expression does not independently inspect the final-wave marker. At level 81, $N=13$ and $s=5$: current wave 4 is guarded; current wave 12 before a short final wave 13 is not guarded by this expression.

Turning the option off leaves normal progression active. Turning it on adds these manual-style requests. Because the trigger is a visibility transition, changing the preference while requested visibility remains true is not itself another such event.<Note id="skipOption" />

<section id="manual-warning" className="prose-section">

## A tap during the warning leaves a different state behind

The pre-spawn huge-wave warning can precede a regular flag wave or the final wave. The ordinary timed update has already requested the next-wave button before it enters announcement state 2, and entering that state does not hide the button. A successful tap reaches the direct advance handler, which rejects only finished state 8. It starts the announced wave immediately and hides the button.

That warning is separate from the final-wave-start message. The latter is emitted when the final wave actually starts, after advance has requested the button hidden. Seeing a banner alone therefore does not establish that the button is still available.

| Action | State after the announced wave starts | What governs its next transition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Let the warning expire | Normal timed state 1 | Ordinary health, deadline, and button checks |
| Tap before the warning starts | Normal timed state 1 | Ordinary health, deadline, and button checks |
| Tap during the warning | Announcement state 2 remains | Fresh deadline; normal HP acceleration and button-show logic are omitted |

The difference is a missing reset on the manual path. The announcement timeout advances the wave and then restores normal timing. A manual request calls advance directly and omits that reset. Starting the new wave still replaces its last-wave time and deadline, so the old warning deadline no longer governs it.<Note id="waveAnnouncement" />

For a controlled example, wave 4 is active and the warning deadline is 35. A tap at 31 starts wave 5. With an entered next interval of 22.5, its fresh deadline is 53.5. Even if the new wave clears at 36, state 2 does not run the usual HP acceleration. At 53.5, wave 6 starts and normal state 1 resumes. Letting the original warning expire instead starts wave 5 at 35 and restores state 1 immediately.

For the final wave, retaining state 2 can likewise postpone completion after the required enemies have cleared. At the fresh deadline, exhausting the wave vector writes finished state 8, but the announcement caller then resets it to state 1. A further normal update leaves it finished. There is no extra scheduled wave, and <Link href="/timing/#victory">the entity-clear predicate</Link> must also pass. The extra update has no fixed duration established by these checks.

The warning tab in the interactive view compares these paths using editable times and a chosen clearance event. It keeps the automatic-next-wave preference off so the manual request can be examined separately. The preference’s regular-boundary guard does not reject a player’s tap.

</section>

<section id="initial-rush" className="prose-section">

## Initial approach speed is a separate option

`HasStartRushOnScreen` is checked during creation together with the type’s `HastyOnStart`, spawn-wave marker, and mode gates. An accepted request applies a condition that doubles movement pace before later adjustments. It clears at game-coordinate $X\le792$.

`HastyOnStart` defaults to true. The current Endless and portal catalog explicitly sets it false for `cannon`, `swashbuckler`, `west_bull`, and `lostcity_relichunter`. Portal marker $-5$ also fails the creation path’s wave-marker gate. Later knockback does not, by this path, reapply the initial condition.<Note id="rushOption" />

This affects how soon eligible entities reach the lawn and how waves overlap. It does not reroll the pool, alter the point budget, or speed up the global game clock.

</section>

<section id="victory" className="prose-section">

## Completion is an AND over its registered predicates

For registered callbacks $c_1,\ldots,c_m$,

$$
C=(m>0)\;\land\;\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}c_j.
$$

The manager rejects an empty callback vector and stops on the first false result. Two required checks here are the finished `WaveManager` and `ZombiesDeadWinCon`. The former becomes finished when advancement reaches the end of the installed wave vector; merely starting the final wave is not that transition.

`ZombiesDeadWinCon` first scans qualifying Board zombie pointers, without restricting them to the current wave. It then scans the relevant grid-item collection for objects whose blocking predicate returns true. Hence earlier survivors, portal children, and the portal itself can still matter after current-wave health has reached its threshold.<Note id="completion" />

A <Link href="/timing/#manual-warning">manual tap during the final pre-spawn warning</Link> can leave the manager waiting for its fresh deadline even after entity clearance. Clearing the lawn alone does not reset the retained announcement state.

Its final emission leaves another scheduled check; that check requests closure, and the close-animation callback removes the portal. An empty queue therefore does not immediately clear that blocker. Surviving children remain independent blockers after the portal disappears.<Note id="portalClose" />

## Loss and continuation inspect different state again

The ordinary loss module requires `CanTriggerZombieWin` and its own state/type/flag gates. In the unshifted branch, its declared `ZombieWinPositionX = -80` means

$$
X-200\le-80\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad X\le120.
$$

A Board flag can shift the threshold by 30, and another mode can add a Y check. A negative spawn-wave marker alone is not an exemption.<Note id="loss" />

After victory, the client sends the end-level request and processes the returned progress record before continuing. It does not simply increment local progress and assume success. The ordinary continuation route loads the next file only when the returned stored progress plus one is below 150. With the bundled Boss interval of 5, the sequence through 1–149 includes 120 ordinary levels and 29 Boss boundaries.<Note id="progression" />

That record is not a saved battlefield. The ordinary definitions declare `CanSaveGameState: false`; neither progression nor the separate <Link href="/drops/#loot-schedule">saved loot schedules</Link> establishes restoration of a previous wave, its entities, or its row history.

</section>
